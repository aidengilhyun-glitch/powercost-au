Air Conditioner vs Fan Running Cost

Fans and air conditioners do different jobs, so a useful comparison needs realistic power, runtime and comfort assumptions.

Why watts alone are not enough

A fan moves air and can improve comfort without cooling the room. An air conditioner actively transfers heat and may control temperature and humidity. Compare the energy needed to achieve an acceptable outcome, not just the appliance name.

Use realistic average input

A fan may have several speed settings. A reverse-cycle air conditioner can vary its electrical input as the compressor modulates. Use measured energy, an applicable energy label or a realistic average rather than assuming maximum watts for every hour.

Calculate both on the same basis

For each appliance, divide watts by 1,000, multiply by hours used and then by the same electricity rate. Keep the number of use days consistent. The difference between the two estimates is not a guaranteed saving because comfort and operating conditions differ.

Consider combined use

In some homes, a fan may help distribute conditioned air or allow a less aggressive thermostat setting. Do not claim a saving without measuring the combined setup under comparable weather and room conditions.

Safety and practical limits

Follow manufacturer instructions, keep vents clear and use qualified help for installation or electrical concerns. Cost estimates should never override heat-health advice or safe indoor temperatures.

General information only

Check your current electricity plan, appliance information and actual operating conditions. PowerCost AU provides estimates, not energy-plan, financial, electrical or health advice.
