Watts vs Kilowatt-Hours Explained

Understand the difference between appliance power in watts and electricity consumption in kilowatt-hours.

Watts measure power

Watts describe the rate at which an appliance uses energy at a moment in time. A 2,000-watt heater has a power input of 2 kilowatts.

Kilowatt-hours measure energy

A kilowatt-hour combines power and time. A 2-kilowatt appliance running for three hours uses 6 kWh.

Why the difference matters

Electricity bills charge for energy in kWh, while appliance labels often show watts. Both the power rating and operating time are needed to estimate cost.

Cycling appliances

Fridges, air conditioners and thermostatic heaters switch or modulate during operation. Their maximum watts multiplied by elapsed time can overestimate actual energy use.
