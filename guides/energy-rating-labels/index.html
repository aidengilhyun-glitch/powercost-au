Understanding Appliance Energy Rating Labels

Use Australian appliance energy information to make better running-cost estimates.

Annual energy consumption

Where an energy label provides estimated annual kWh, multiply that figure by your electricity rate in dollars per kWh for a simple annual usage-cost estimate.

Star ratings

Star ratings help compare efficiency within the relevant product category and test standard. They do not directly state your household's exact cost.

Label assumptions

Test conditions and assumed usage may differ from your home. Climate, settings, load, maintenance and household behaviour can all change real consumption.

Choosing the best input

For annual estimates, an applicable energy-label kWh figure is often more useful than multiplying maximum watts by every hour the appliance is switched on.

Worked example

If a refrigerator label states 320 kWh per year and your usage rate is 34 cents per kWh, the estimated annual usage cost is 320 × $0.34 = $108.80. Add supply charges only when comparing the whole electricity bill; do not assign the full household supply charge to one appliance.

Labels, watts and real-world use

The label's annual kWh is measured under a specified test method, while a watt rating describes power at a particular operating point. Read watts versus kilowatt-hours before converting between the two. For a personal estimate, enter the label's annual kWh and your current tariff in the main calculator, and compare alternatives with the comparison tool.

Related appliance calculators

Use the dedicated refrigerator calculator, clothes-dryer calculator or air-conditioner calculator when you know how often the appliance runs. These tools make the usage assumptions explicit so you can adjust them instead of treating a label estimate as a guarantee.

Use the result carefully
