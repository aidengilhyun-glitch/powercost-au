Standby Power Cost: How to Estimate It

A small continuous load can add up over a full year. Use watts, hours and your tariff to estimate the cost before deciding what to switch off.

The standby formula

Standby watts ÷ 1,000 × 24 hours × 365 days gives annual kWh. Multiply that energy by your usage rate in dollars per kWh for the estimated annual cost.

Example calculation

A device drawing 5 W continuously uses about 43.8 kWh per year. At 32 cents per kWh, that is about $14.02 per year. Actual standby draw and tariffs vary, so replace both example values with your own.

Measure before changing habits

Some devices draw almost nothing in standby while others remain network-connected or keep displays and power supplies active. A plug-in energy meter is useful for suitable plug-in devices. Never disconnect equipment needed for safety, medical use, security, updates or essential connectivity.

Focus on groups of devices

One tiny load may have little effect, but several entertainment, office or kitchen devices can be assessed together. A switched power board may be convenient only where the manufacturer permits full disconnection.
