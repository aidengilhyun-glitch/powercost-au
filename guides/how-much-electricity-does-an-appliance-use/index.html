How Much Electricity Does an Appliance Use?

Turn an appliance's watt rating and running time into kilowatt-hours (kWh), the energy unit shown on electricity bills.

Start with watts and runtime

Find the input power on the appliance label or manual. Divide watts by 1,000 to convert to kilowatts, then multiply by the number of hours used. A 1,200 W appliance used for 2 hours consumes an estimated 2.4 kWh.

Daily, monthly and annual use

Multiply daily kWh by the number of use days. For a regular daily appliance, use 30.4 days for an average month or 365 days for a year. For occasional appliances, calculate from realistic weekly use instead.

Rated power is not always average power

Fridges, air conditioners, heat pumps and thermostatic heaters cycle or vary their output. Multiplying maximum rated watts by every switched-on hour can overstate consumption. An energy label, smart plug or plug-in energy meter can provide a better input.

Convert energy use to cost

Multiply kWh by your electricity usage rate in dollars per kWh. Do not include the daily supply charge when estimating the incremental cost of one appliance, because that fixed charge usually applies whether the appliance runs or not.

Check the result

Treat the number as an estimate. Compare it with an energy label or measured consumption where available, and use the exact tariff that applies at the time of operation.
